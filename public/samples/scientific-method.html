The Scientific Method: A Step-by-Step Guide

How scientists turn questions into reliable knowledge — the six steps, variables and controls, and why replication matters.

The scientific method is a systematic approach to answering questions about the natural world. Instead of relying on intuition or tradition, scientists form testable explanations, gather evidence, and refine their understanding based on what the data shows.

The Six Steps

  1. Ask a question about an observation.
  2. Do background research on what is already known.
  3. Form a hypothesis — a testable, falsifiable prediction.
  4. Test the hypothesis with a controlled experiment.
  5. Analyze the data and draw conclusions.
  6. Share results so others can replicate and build on them.

Variables and Controls

A well-designed experiment changes exactly one thing at a time. The variable the researcher deliberately changes is the independent variable; the variable that is measured is the dependent variable. Everything else is held constant.

  • Independent variable — what you change.
  • Dependent variable — what you measure.
  • Controlled variables — what you keep the same.
  • Control group — a baseline with no treatment, used for comparison.
⚠️ Common pitfall: correlation is not causation. Two things happening together does not prove that one caused the other.

Why Replication Matters

A single study is rarely the final word. Results must be reproducible — other researchers should be able to follow the same procedure and get similar findings. Replication filters out flukes, errors, and false positives, which is how scientific knowledge becomes trustworthy over time.

Flashcards

What is a hypothesis?

A testable, falsifiable prediction that can be supported or rejected by an experiment.

What is the independent variable?

The variable the researcher deliberately changes to test its effect.

What is the dependent variable?

The variable that is measured to see whether the independent variable had an effect.

What is a control group?

A baseline group kept under standard conditions, used as a comparison against the experimental group.

Why is reproducibility important?

Results that can be repeated by others are more trustworthy and less likely to be flukes or errors.

What is a scientific theory?

A well-tested explanation of natural phenomena, supported by a large body of evidence.

Quiz yourself

A researcher tests whether more sleep improves test scores. What is the independent variable?

Hours of sleep. The independent variable is what the researcher changes.

Which statement is a proper hypothesis?

"Students who study 30 minutes daily will score at least 10% higher on exams than students who do not." A hypothesis must be specific, testable and falsifiable.

A hypothesis that cannot be proven wrong is still scientifically useful. True or false?

False. If a claim cannot be disproven, it cannot be tested scientifically.

What is the purpose of a control group?

To provide a baseline for comparison with the experimental group.

If a result is statistically significant, it is definitely important in the real world. True or false?

False. Significance means the result is unlikely to be chance; real-world importance depends on effect size and context.
