Forces and Motion: Newton's Three Laws

Force, mass and acceleration — what they mean, how they're measured, and the three laws that connect them.

A force is a push or a pull acting on an object. Forces are measured in newtons (N) and always have both a size and a direction, which makes them vectors. Motion changes only when the forces on an object are unbalanced — and exactly how they change is described by Isaac Newton's three laws of motion, first published in 1687.

Newton's First Law — Inertia

An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion at a constant speed in a straight line, unless acted on by an unbalanced force. This resistance to change in motion is called inertia.

  • A book on a table stays put until you push it.
  • Passengers lurch forward when a bus brakes suddenly — their bodies keep moving while the bus slows.

Newton's Second Law — F = ma

The acceleration of an object depends on the net force acting on it and its mass: F = m × a. The bigger the force, the bigger the acceleration; the bigger the mass, the smaller the acceleration for the same force.

  • Pushing an empty shopping cart accelerates it easily; a full cart needs much more force for the same acceleration.
  • It's harder to kick a heavy ball than a light one — mass resists acceleration.
⚖️ Mass vs. weight: mass is the amount of matter in an object (kilograms) and never changes; weight is the gravitational force on that mass (newtons) and changes with location — you'd weigh less on the Moon but have the same mass.

Newton's Third Law — Action and Reaction

For every action force, there is an equal and opposite reaction force. Forces always come in pairs acting on different objects.

  • When you jump, you push down on the ground and the ground pushes you up with equal force.
  • A rocket pushes exhaust gases backward, and the gases push the rocket forward.

Friction and Gravity

Two forces dominate everyday motion. Friction opposes motion between surfaces in contact and converts kinetic energy into heat — it's why a sliding box eventually stops. Gravity is the attractive force between masses; near Earth's surface it accelerates objects downward at about 9.8 m/s².

Flashcards

What is a force?

A push or a pull acting on an object, measured in newtons, with both size and direction.

What is inertia?

An object's resistance to changes in its motion — the tendency of Newton's first law.

State Newton's second law.

F = m × a: acceleration equals net force divided by mass.

State Newton's third law.

For every action force there is an equal and opposite reaction force, acting on a different object.

What is the difference between mass and weight?

Mass is the amount of matter (kg, constant); weight is the gravitational force on that mass (N, location-dependent).

What does friction do?

It opposes motion between surfaces in contact and turns kinetic energy into heat.

Quiz yourself

A ball rolls across a floor and gradually stops. Which force is mainly responsible?

Friction opposes the motion and converts kinetic energy into heat.

What is the SI unit of force?

The newton (N).

Two objects are pushed with the same force. The more massive object accelerates…

Less. With F = m × a, a larger mass gives a smaller acceleration for the same force.

When you push on a wall, the wall pushes back with equal force. Which law is this?

Newton's third law — action and reaction forces act on different objects.
