Turning Lecture Slides into Study Material

Slides are outlines, not knowledge. Here's how to turn a deck into something you actually understand — before, during, and after the lecture.

Before: preview the deck

If the slides are posted before class, spend ten minutes skimming them. Don't try to learn anything — just build a mental map: what are the main topics, which terms keep appearing, and what looks hardest? Lectures land differently when your brain already has hooks to hang them on. Write down two or three questions the lecture should answer.

During: annotate, don't transcribe

The slides are the handout — you don't need to copy them. Your job during the lecture is to capture what's not on the slides: explanations, examples, emphasis ("this will be on the exam"), and the answers to your preview questions. One useful trick: for each slide, write a single sentence in your own words that captures its point. Slides you can't summarize in one sentence are slides you haven't understood yet.

After: convert within 24 hours

This is where slides become study material. Go through the deck once and, for each slide, ask: what question does this slide answer? Write that question down. You've just converted a passive deck into an active recall bank. The same conversion is exactly what AIstudy does automatically: import the deck and it produces structured notes, flashcards and a quiz from the content.

📊 Messy decks? Image-heavy or dense decks work fine — the key content is usually in the titles, bullet text and speaker notes. Combine the deck with your lecture annotations for the best output.

The full loop

  1. Preview → write 2–3 questions.
  2. Attend → capture explanations and examples.
  3. Convert → questions, flashcards, and notes from the deck.
  4. Review → spaced repetition over the following weeks.
