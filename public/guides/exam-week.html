How to Study for an Exam in One Week

Seven days is plenty — if every day has a job. This plan trades panic for a daily cycle of diagnose, recall, and test.

The shape of the week

  • Day 1 — Diagnose. List every topic. For each, answer one question honestly: could I explain this from memory right now? Sort into solid, shaky, and unknown. Your plan now writes itself: most time goes to unknown, some to shaky, almost none to solid.
  • Days 2–4 — Build and recall. Take the unknown topics two or three per day. For each: read once, then close everything and write a summary from memory, then check. Convert the topic into flashcards or questions and answer them aloud. End each day with a short mixed quiz over everything so far.
  • Day 5 — Practice test. Full-length, exam-style, no notes, timed. The score matters less than the pattern of errors — every miss points at a topic for day 6.
  • Day 6 — Target the gaps. Attack exactly the topics the practice test exposed. Short recall cycles, one topic at a time. This is the highest-value study day of the week.
  • Day 7 — Light touch and rest. A gentle sweep of the shaky list only, plus your flashcard due-queue. No new material. Sleep early — consolidation happens in sleep, and you've been building toward it all week.
🎯 Pareto's law, exam edition: roughly 80% of your marks come from 20% of the material. Days 5–6 are how you find out which 20% matters for you.

Rules that keep the plan alive

  • No all-nighters. A tired brain retrieves poorly and consolidates nothing. Every plan here assumes 7–8 hours of sleep.
  • Recall over reread. If a session doesn't involve producing answers from memory, it isn't a study session.
  • Mix topics. Interleave subjects in your quizzes so retrieval, not recognition, is being trained.
  • Keep sessions under 60 minutes, with real breaks. Attention is a resource; spend it on recall, not scrolling.
